### Act 1: Problem Space & Needfinding Research

Mathematics is frequently perceived by middle and high school students as abstract and intimidating—an attitude reflected in **INVALSI 2024** proficiency gaps, **ISTAT** educational disparity metrics, and **PNRR** digital-learning analyses.

To ground our product decisions in real user behavior rather than assumptions, we combined secondary pedagogical research with **5 semi-structured interviews** across both **immediate/lead users** (3 students aged 12, 15, and 18 across middle school, *liceo scientifico*, and technical institute) and **extreme users** (2 math teachers aged 30 and 50).

From thematic affinity mapping, **4 core user needs** drove every subsequent design and engineering choice:
1. **Mitigate Study Distractions:** Students lose focus during unstructured home practice (often stalling within ~20 minutes); short, timed quiz sprints create clear concentration boundaries.
2. **Gamified Motivation & Engagement:** Turning problem solving into collectible XP, milestone badges, and peer classroom competition transforms anxiety into positive reinforcement.
3. **Structured Progress Visibility:** Learners need granular topic-by-topic error reviews and explanations—not just a final pass/fail score.
4. **Curated Study Repository:** Teachers and advanced peers need a trustworthy way to share study materials directly linked to curriculum topics.

#### Strategic Pivot & Heuristic Resolutions
Based on heuristic feedback and target audience portability habits, we committed to a **Mobile-First Architecture** and resolved key usability violations prior to high-fidelity implementation:
* **Timer & Progress Clarity (H1):** Added an explicit per-question countdown indicator alongside an overall question progress bar (`1/10`).
* **Direct Affordances over Hidden Menus (H2/H4):** Replaced hidden three-dot overflow menus inside active quizzes with explicit action buttons and confirmation dialogs when exiting or uploading.
* **Level-Gated Trust System:** Replaced arbitrary locked/unlocked states with a transparent **Level &ge; 10 Verification Gate** empowering proven learners to contribute community study notes.

#### 1. Unidirectional State & Nested Stack Navigation
The client architecture uses `useReducer` paired with React `StateContext` (`Reducer.ts` / `Action.ts`) and `PreferencesContext` (MD3 Light/Dark theme toggling), decoupled from a clean nested navigator (`RootStack` -> `BottomTabNavigator` with independent `HomeStack`, `MaterialeStack`, `ProfileStack`, and modal fullscreen `Play` gameplay).

#### 2. Timed Quiz Engine, Tactical Bonuses & XP Calculation
Inside `PlayScreen.tsx`, each question tracks elapsed response time against `timerDuration`, supports math rendering via `react-native-math-view` and `MathJax`, and grants three single-use power-ups per challenge: **`+30 secondi`** (resets timer progress by 50%), **`Doppio tentativo`** (grants a second attempt on a wrong answer without advancing), and **`Mostra Grafico`** (reveals visual function plots from `GraficList`).

Upon completion, XP scales dynamically by challenge difficulty, accuracy, and speed bonus, rolling over every **5,000 XP**:

```ts
// Speed-weighted XP scoring & atomic Firebase RTDB persistence (Mathify/pages/PlayScreen.tsx)
const correctAnswers = risposte.filter((risposta) => risposta === true).length;
const xpCorretto = 10 * correctAnswers;

const tempoTotXP = listaTempoDomande.reduce((sum, num, index) => {
    const numArrotondato = Math.round(timerDuration - num);
    return sum + (numArrotondato * (risposte[index] ? 1 : 0));
}, 0);

const totaleXP = state.selectedChallenge.difficolta * 50 + xpCorretto + tempoTotXP;
registraSfidaCompletata(sfidaCompletata, state.selectedChallenge);
aggiornaLivello(sfidaCompletata.xpGuadagnati);
```

#### 3. Crowd-Sourced QA & Level-Gated Community Materials
Students can flag ambiguous wording or incorrect solutions inline (`registraSegnalazione()`), writing structured `DomandaSegnalata` records directly to Firebase so contributors can inspect unresolved reports in `Segnalazioni.tsx`. Meanwhile, reaching **Level 10** unlocks account verification (`registraVerifica()`), allowing verified students to publish custom math sheets into the `Materiale` hub.

### Act 4: Cooperative Usability Testing & Quantitative Results

We conducted **cooperative usability testing sessions** with **4 target-age participants** (11–19 years old across middle school, *liceo scientifico*, and vocational high school). Each student performed **10 end-to-end tasks** (running timed quizzes with bonuses, reviewing wrong explanations, flagging a buggy question, checking level status, completing account verification, uploading materials, and checking the classroom podium) followed by the **System Usability Scale (SUS)** questionnaire.

| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Test 1 — Silvia** | 18 · *Liceo Scientifico* | 10 / 10 | **90.0 / 100** | Rated quiz flow highly intuitive; suggested making Verification easier to locate. |
| **Test 2 — Alessandro** | 19 · *Istituto Alberghiero* | 10 / 10 | **72.5 / 100** | Appreciated profile graphics; recommended larger leaderboard podium & richer colors. |
| **Test 3 — Giada** | 19 · *Istituto Alberghiero* | 10 / 10 | **82.5 / 100** | Navigated independently across all 10 tasks; suggested clearer podium chart styling. |
| **Test 4 — Abkari** | 11 · *Scuola Media* | 10 / 10 | **72.5 / 100** | Grasped quiz gameplay immediately; asked for higher color contrast on key controls. |
| **Aggregate** | **4 Target Students** | **40 / 40 (100%)** | **79.4 / 100 Avg** | **Zero critical failures**; guided final color variety, podium hierarchy & save toast fixes. |

#### Prototype Scope & Transparent Limitations
As an academic functional prototype, **3 core challenges**, XP/level rollover (`5,000 XP/level`), completed challenge history, earned badges, question reports (`Segnalazioni`), and account verification sync dynamically with Firebase Realtime Database, while peripheral explore cards, the mock classroom leaderboard (`Classe.tsx`), scheduled challenges (`SfideProgrammate.tsx`), and non-verification settings items use local placeholder data. *(Note: the mobile app UI and quiz bank are written in Italian.)*
