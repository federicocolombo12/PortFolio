As a Technical Artist, one of my main goals is removing friction from the environment art pipeline. When building sci-fi or cyberpunk environments, artists spend hours manually placing bezier curves to simulate hanging cables and power lines. This process is tedious and highly destructive if the environment layout changes.

I built the **Smart Cable Generator**, a Python `bpy` add-on for Blender, to fully automate this workflow. Here is a breakdown of the engineering decisions and iterations behind the tool.

### Iteration 1: The Surface Crawler vs. Mid-Air Reality
Initially, I designed the script using a custom **Dijkstra shortest-path algorithm** via the `bmesh` API. The idea was to select two points and have the cable crawl perfectly along the topology of the walls.

However, after testing it in a real production scenario, I realized a critical flaw: environments are built using modular, disconnected meshes. If an artist wants to connect an antenna on Building A to an AC unit on Building B, a topology-based algorithm fails. I pivoted to a **Mid-Air Mathematical approach**, relying on global coordinate matrices (`matrix_world @ v.co`) to draw curves freely through 3D space, independent of the underlying geometry.

### Iteration 2: Scale-Independent Parabolic Gravity
To simulate gravity without the immense performance cost of actual physics simulations, I implemented a parametric parabolic curve equation: `4.0 * gravity_sag * t * (1.0 - t)`.

During testing, I noticed that a static gravity offset looked great on small props but was invisible on massive skyscrapers. I updated the math to be **proportional to the distance** between the two selected points (`distance = (p1 - p2).length`). Now, a 10% sag looks physically accurate regardless of whether the points are 10 centimeters or 1 kilometer apart.

### Iteration 3: Making it "Production Ready" (V2.0)
To transform the script from a neat trick into a production-ready pipeline tool, I built a native UI panel into Blender's sidebar and added features tailored for Game Engine export:

1. **Organic Bundle Generation:** Real cables don't hang perfectly parallel. I added a `Cable Count` slider that uses pseudo-random noise to apply slight X/Y offsets and gravity variations. This instantly generates messy, organic bundles.
2. **Material Auto-Assignment:** Artists can pick a material directly from the UI, skipping the tedious step of assigning rubber shaders to 100 individual curves.
3. **Auto-Convert for FBX:** Blender curves don't export cleanly to Unreal Engine. I added an "Auto-Convert to Mesh" toggle that programmatically switches context, applies the spline geometry, and prepares game-ready polygons for one-click FBX export.
