This is my personal sandbox. I built this **C++ / OpenGL** rendering framework from scratch because I wanted to step away from commercial engines (Unity/Unreal) for a bit and actually understand what happens under the hood. 

The goal isn't to build the next Unreal Engine, but to get my hands dirty with the rendering equation, memory architecture, and the GPU pipeline. I test everything using a stylistic approach inspired by my favorite anime just to keep things fun.

### Tech Stack
- **C++17** & **OpenGL 3.3 Core**
- **GLFW** (Window/Input)
- **GLM** (Math)
- **Assimp** (Model Loading)
- **CMake**

---

### What's working so far

#### 1. Deferred Rendering (G-Buffer)
I skipped Forward Rendering entirely and went straight for a Deferred Shading pipeline. The engine populates a G-Buffer (Albedo, Normals, Metallic/Roughness, Depth) first, then processes lighting. This lets me throw a ton of dynamic lights into the scene without tanking the framerate.

#### 2. Physically Based Rendering (PBR)
I implemented the Cook-Torrance BRDF. The lighting model respects energy conservation based on roughness and metallic maps.

#### 3. Raymarching & SDFs
I integrated a Signed Distance Field (SDF) system directly into the pipeline. I'm using Raymarching to render infinite fractals and boolean operations that would be a nightmare to do with standard polygons.

### Roadmap (What I'm building next)

This is very much a work in progress. Here is what is on my immediate to-do list:

**Geometry & Shading:**
- **Silhouette Parallax Occlusion Mapping (SPOM):** Tangent-space raymarching with silhouette clipping to give textures actual depth (similar to what they did in *Crimson Desert*).
- **NPR / Toon Shading:** Tweaking the PBR model to get a Cel Shaded look with dynamic outlines.

**Lighting & Screen Space:**
- **Image Based Lighting (IBL):** Setting up HDRI Skyboxes with Irradiance and Pre-Filtered Environment Maps.
- **Shadow Mapping:** Directional shadows are next on the list.
- **SSAO & HDR/Bloom:** Screen space contact shadows and Gaussian blur for emissive materials.

**Compute Shaders:**
- **Water Simulation:** I want to move ocean rendering (Gerstner Waves/FFT) to the GPU via Compute Shaders.
- **GPGPU Particles:** GPU-driven particle systems for smoke and magical effects.

### Debugging & Future-proofing
I rely heavily on **RenderDoc** to profile draw calls and inspect the G-Buffer attachments frame by frame. Right now the architecture is tied to OpenGL, but I'm structuring the codebase so I can eventually port the rendering backend to **Vulkan** or **D3D12**.
