This Unreal Engine 5 Technical Art study focused on building a non-destructive open environment—blending grounded dark-fantasy terrain with stylized foliage and atmospheric lighting—without relying on manual sculpting or hand-placed props.

### 1. Heightmap Ingestion & Rule-Driven Auto-Materials

Instead of sculpting terrain manually, I imported a 16-bit heightmap (generated via Gaea / Motion Forge) into the UE5 Landscape system, calibrating Z-scale ratios so cliff proportions held up cleanly in-engine.

I then authored a **3-rule Landscape Auto-Material**:
- **Slope Masks (World Normal Z):** Blends grass across flat ground and exposes cliff rock automatically past threshold angles.
- **Altitude Masks:** Applies snow transition strictly above a target world Z-height.
- **Macro/Micro Tiling Breakup:** Layers multi-scale noise masks over distance to break visible texture repetition across wide terrain sweeps.

### 2. Spline-Driven PCG & Nanite Foliage

Layout elements (paths, fences, clearing zones, and foliage density) are governed by **PCG graphs reading level splines**. Moving a spline updates surrounding props and vegetation scatter without manual rework.

For tree canopies, standard billboard LODs introduced harsh pop-in and flat shading, so I switched to **Nanite Megaplants** with Subsurface Scattering (SSS) and World Position Offset (WPO) wind shaders.

### 3. Time-of-Day Integration & Roadblocks

Lighting integrates the **Celestial Night** sky plugin alongside Exponential Height Fog and Volumetric Clouds driven by a shared time variable, paired with a custom **Switching Lights Blueprint** that toggles tagged street lamps automatically as sun elevation drops past dusk.

**Roadblocks Encountered:**
- **Nanite Landscape Instabilities:** Enabling Nanite directly on the full Landscape triggered severe frame drops and editor crashes on this hardware target, forcing a fallback to standard Landscape LOD tessellation paired with Nanite strictly on foliage/meshes.
- **Megaplant PCG Instancing & Mie Scattering:** Megaplant assemblies required custom PCG spawning filters compared to single static meshes, and misconfigured Volumetric Fog Mie Scattering initially flooded scenes with bright white speckling resembling snowfall.
