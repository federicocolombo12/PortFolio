This UE5 Technical Art module ("Post Mortem", built in collaboration with teammate Dario) tackled getting custom facial identities, custom hair grooms, and budget motion capture working together cleanly inside **Unreal Engine 5 Sequencer**.

### 1. Why the First Pipeline Broke: FaceBuilder & Groom Bindings

Our first attempt used **Blender FaceBuilder** with camera matching and texture projection. When imported into UE5, the *MetaHuman Identity* solver re-fitted its base topology over the custom sculpt, flattening distinct facial features.

Custom hair built with Blender's *Hair Curves System* and exported via Alembic (`.abc`) also failed in-engine:

- **Broken Groom Binding:** During facial deformation, hair roots lost tracking against the skeletal mesh and clipped or detached across the face.
- **Coordinate Scale Trap:** Applying object scale in Blender prior to Alembic export corrupted UE5's groom binding transform scale during physics evaluation.

### 2. Pivot to RealityScan + Mesh-to-MetaHuman

Instead of forcing third-party topology, we scanned ourselves with **RealityScan** and ran **Mesh to MetaHuman**. Keeping inside UE5's native rig preserved skin shaders, facial control rigs, and groom bindings; tuning **Strand Size** in the Groom asset stopped the remaining root collisions during dialogue.

### 3. Low-Budget MoCap & Sequencer Assembly

We split the 7-shot sequence (spanning 12mm wide interiors to 200mm exteriors and 85mm close-ups) across modular **Sequencer Subsequences** so animation cleanup and camera cuts stayed independent:
- **Body Motion (`Cartwheel`):** Corrected foot sliding and drifting root motion in Sequencer using *Additive Animation Tracks*.
- **Facial Capture (`Live Link Face`):** Recorded iPhone ARKit blendshapes directly onto the MetaHuman control rig.
