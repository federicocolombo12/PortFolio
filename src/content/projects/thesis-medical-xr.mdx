**Bio-Adaptive Signed Distance Fields in Medical XR** is my ongoing Master Thesis project in collaboration with the *Lemons In The Room* startup. It focuses on pushing the boundaries of procedural generation and physiological computing on standalone XR headsets.

## The Challenge
The goal is to develop a dynamic, bio-responsive visual medium for trauma therapy in Extended Reality. Instead of relying on static pre-baked meshes, the project maps real-time physiological data (ECG, Heart Rate, SpO2) from medical devices directly onto the mathematical parameters of Signed Distance Fields (SDFs). This creates a "Bio-Adaptive Visual System" that mirrors and facilitates a patient's autonomic regulation.

## The Research Pivot: From Environmental Mesh Reconstruction to Bio-Adaptive Raymarching
The thesis initially started with a different focus: **Real-time Mesh Reconstruction** and environmental depth scanning for Mixed Reality. The early experiments involved raw depth extraction using Meta's Depth API, depth map reprojection, and point cloud generation. 

However, during the research phase, I discovered robust open-source frameworks on GitHub (specifically `QuestRoomScan` and `UnityGaussianSplatting`) that already solved the environmental scanning and reconstruction problem out-of-the-box. Realizing that reinventing environmental mesh reconstruction wouldn't add enough novel value, I made a strategic decision to pivot the research entirely.

The new (and final) direction became **Bio-Adaptive SDFs via Pure Mathematical Raymarching (HLSL)**. Instead of reconstructing static real-world meshes, the project shifted to generating dynamic, infinite-resolution bio-feedback shapes that morph in real-time without the overhead of traditional polygonal rendering.

## Core Technical Art Pillars

### 1. Procedural Topological Morphing
Instead of traditional mesh morphing, I am developing a C#/HLSL framework for **Runtime SDFs**. By calculating distance fields mathematically on the GPU, the system achieves infinite, seamless morphing states—such as blending a high-frequency "stress" geometry into a smooth "calm" sphere—without memory overhead or voxelization artifacts.

### 2. High-Performance Raymarching for Mobile XR
A major hurdle for Raymarching in XR is the immense GPU fill-rate cost, which typically makes it unviable for standalone headsets like the Meta Quest 3 (which requires 72Hz+ for medical safety). I am implementing advanced optimization techniques:
- **Dynamic Bounding Volume Adapting**: Adjusting the raymarching volume bounds dynamically to fit the active SDF nodes, drastically reducing pixel evaluation.
- **Foveated Raymarching**: Rendering peripheral rays at lower resolutions by integrating deeply with the Scriptable Render Pipeline (URP/HDRP).

### 3. Bio-Feedback Hardware Integration
I established a BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy) bridge between Unity and the "Checkme Pro" medical device. This pipeline streams raw physiological data, passes it through interpolation buffers to ensure jitter-free geometric deformations, and feeds it directly into the shader properties.

## Unity Asset Store Potential
A primary objective of this research is productization. The final framework will be packaged into a commercial-grade, VR-optimized tool for the **Unity Asset Store**, filling a major market gap for modern, high-performance raymarching tools optimized for URP/HDRP. It will feature Custom Nodes for Unity Shader Graph, allowing non-programmers to visually customize lighting, PBR shading, and textures on mathematically defined surfaces.
