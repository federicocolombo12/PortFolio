**PulseEngine** is a real-time rendering and telemetry engine that bridges continuous biometric data streams (ECG, blood oxygenation, autonomic stress) with procedural computer graphics in Extended Reality. 

Instead of traditional mesh skinning or static polygonal assets, PulseEngine translates human physiology into **living, mathematical Signed Distance Fields (SDFs)** and **massive GPU particle swarms**. To manage continuous multi-sensor telemetry without messy spaghetti code, I designed a **Virtual DAW Mixer Console** inside Unity, borrowing workflows directly from professional audio workstations like Ableton Live.

## 🧭 The Research Pivot: From Room Scanning to Pure Math

My thesis initially started with real-time environmental depth scanning for Mixed Reality on Meta Quest 3, testing raw depth extraction via Meta's Depth API and point cloud reprojection. 

Early on, I realized open-source frameworks like `QuestRoomScan` and `UnityGaussianSplatting` already tackled environmental reconstruction effectively. Reinventing that wheel offered little novelty. I decided to pivot the research toward an unsolved problem: **how to represent dynamic, living biological forms in mobile XR without polygon limits or mesh artifacts**.

By shifting to **Pure Mathematical Raymarching (HLSL)**, the visual medium gained infinite topological freedom. A heart or cellular organism can morph, divide, swell, and melt seamlessly—all computed mathematically on the GPU.

## 🎛️ The Virtual DAW Mixer: Audio Paradigms for Visual Telemetry

Connecting multi-protocol telemetry (OSC, WebSockets, Serial microcontrollers, BLE medical sensors) directly to scene objects usually creates unmaintainable chaos. To solve this, I built the **PulseEngine DAW Mixer** (`DAWMixerWindow`).

Inspired by professional digital audio workstations, every biosignal has its own hardware-style channel strip with interactive faders, real-time OLED oscilloscopes, and VU meters. Clinicians or technical artists can hit **Solo [S]** or **Mute [M]** to isolate a specific biomarker or silence an erratic sensor without dropping network packets. An integrated signal sniffer auto-detects incoming packet keys and maps them in one click, while complete states serialize into ScriptableObject snapshots for immediate clinical recall.

## 🧬 Procedural CSG & Solving the 180° Symmetry Seam Bug

The volumetric engine evaluates Constructive Solid Geometry (CSG) entirely on the GPU. Taking cues from tools like MudBun, shapes are organized hierarchically via Unity sibling indices: Smooth Union, Smooth Subtraction, and Smooth Intersection are accumulated top-to-bottom with per-node blend softness. Standalone modifier volumes (`SdfModifierVolume`) apply localized field distortions—like twists, bends, and bounded 3D noise—with double-wireframe gizmos indicating the inner core and falloff margin.

Because implicit surfaces don't have traditional UVs, texturing required an object-space triplanar projection pipeline. A major technical hurdle here was normal mapping: standard triplanar implementations use Cartesian sign flips, which causes glaring 180° normal inversions and ugly seams across symmetry planes (X=0, Y=0, Z=0). 

To fix this, I implemented the **Morten Mikkelsen Surface Gradient** formulation (`∇_surf(h) = ∇h - (N · ∇h)N`). Because the surface gradient is strictly tangent to the implicit normal, it guarantees continuous C¹ micro-relief across organic morphing shapes with zero symmetry seams. Paired with view-space MatCap shading and dynamic URP directional lighting, this gives clinical organic surfaces the look of living tissue or anatomical bone.

## ⚡ The 90 FPS VR Optimization: Physics-Only Mode

Full-screen raymarching at standalone VR resolutions (2064 × 2208 per eye on Meta Quest 3) is notorious for destroying GPU fill-rates. To achieve guaranteed 90 Hz framerates for clinical safety, I engineered a high-performance **GPU Voxelizer** (`SdfVolumeVoxelizer`) paired with Unity Visual Effect Graph.

Every frame, a Compute Shader evaluates the entire CSG hierarchy and modifier stack over an 8×8×8 thread grid, baking a 128³ RGBAHalf 3D RenderTexture in under **0.8 milliseconds**. The red channel holds metric signed distance for particle adhesion, while the green, blue, and alpha channels store interpolated surface colors for dynamic cellular tinting.

This architecture unlocked **Physics-Only Mode**: on standalone headsets, the expensive fragment raymarching pass can be completely turned off. The SDF continues to voxelize into the 3D texture, allowing millions of particles to adhere, swirl, and collide against the invisible mathematical surface with **zero screen fragment raymarching overhead**.

## 🧠 Affective Biofeedback & Cross-Modal Perception: The Bouba-Kiki Paradigm

In somatic psychotherapy and trauma therapy, patients often struggle with **interoception**—the ability to perceive and interpret their own internal nervous system state. 

To turn invisible autonomic biomarkers into intuitive somatic perception, PulseEngine grounds its biofeedback engine in the cross-modal neurological **Bouba/Kiki effect** (Ramachandran & Hubbard, 2001; Köhler, 1929) and Polyvagal Theory, developed in collaboration with **Politecnico di Torino** and **Lemons in the Room**.
