This R&D benchmark compared classic **Structure-from-Motion (COLMAP)**, **NeRF**, and **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** for turning raw handheld room video into usable interiors inside Unreal Engine 5.

### 1. Capture Protocol & COLMAP Failure Recovery

Hand-held footage introduces rolling-shutter warp and excessive temporal overlap if processed raw:
- **Capture Rules:** Disabled electronic stabilization (EIS) so intrinsics remained consistent, walked room perimeters at chest height, and recorded slow 360° center sweeps.
- **Fixing 10-Hour SfM Stalls:** Feeding all 4,500 extracted video frames directly into local **COLMAP** ran for 10 hours without convergence. Writing an automated `FFmpeg` subsampling script (`1 in 7` frames -> ~640 frames) and executing COLMAP via CLI solved sparse alignment reliably.

### 2. Local XScene vs. Cloud 3DGS inside UE5

Comparing output quality and practical iteration speed for interior spaces:
- **NeRF:** Produced soft "melted wax" geometry and floating view-dependent haze inside tight rooms.
- **Local XScene (UE 5.5 plugin):** After ~4 hours of local compute on an RTX 4070, the output remained noisy and fragmented with heavy boundary artifacts.
- **Cloud Training (`Luma` / `Vid2Scene`) -> `NanoGS`:** Slower queue turnaround, but `.ply` Gaussian splats imported cleanly into UE5 via `NanoGS` with crisp interior edges and material response.
