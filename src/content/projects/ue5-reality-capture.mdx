**Reality Capture** is an exploration of extracting high-fidelity 3D data from 2D videos, comparing traditional photogrammetry with modern neural rendering techniques like NeRFs and Gaussian Splatting.

### 1. Photogrammetry (COLMAP)
Extracting 3D data from video required strict photographic rules. Our "golden rules" were: 
* **No Stabilization**: the camera must be "pure" to avoid confusing the algorithms.
* **Chest Height**: camera facing forward to maintain a constant perspective.
* **Crab Walk**: moving around the perimeter of the room first, followed by three slow 360° pans from the center.

Processing 4500 frames through **COLMAP** locally led to a 10-hour failure due to non-convergent algorithms. We bypassed this by scripting an *FFmpeg* batch to sample frames (1 out of 7), and running COLMAP fully automated via CLI.

### 2. Local vs Cloud Gaussian Splatting
We compared **NeRF** (which suffered from "melted wax" artifacts and floaters) against **Gaussian Splatting**. 

To generate Splats locally, we used **XScene**, a plugin for Unreal Engine 5.5. While it offered complete privacy and process customization without web waitlists, the performance was lacking. After 4 hours of elaboration on an RTX 4070, the resulting mesh was nearly unreadable, heavily corrupted, and impossible to clean up effectively.

As a post-mortem pivot, we tested AI Cloud tools like **Luma** and **Vid2Scene**. Despite having to wait in server queues (taking up to two days), the final `.ply` point clouds worked flawlessly *out-of-the-box* via NanoGS plugins, delivering an incredibly clean and faithful environment reconstruction compared to the local XScene attempt.
